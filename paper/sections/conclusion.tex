\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Comparisons of predictive representation learners that use simulator branching should charge every branch step to the same transition budget as ordinary interaction. They should count restores as a separate privilege, report compute separately from experience, and read representations out through a restore-free pool. We implemented such an accounting in a small aliased T-maze. We showed that a fixed frozen-encoder readout detects cleanly available clue information, and that full-action success is confounded by stalling. In a 2$\times$2 study at \StudyCfgPretrainBudget{} pretraining transitions per collection method, neither action-conditional nor action-free pretraining, with or without branching, improved forced-commit accuracy over an untrained encoder in any of three seeds. The study therefore leaves the question of how branching and action conditioning separate unanswered at this budget. Answering it needs learned encoders that clear the untrained baseline first, and comparisons against stronger, external action-conditioned methods.
