\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the current evidence supports.}
The accounting and information-access properties hold in our implementation, as checked by unit tests. These are technical checks, not evidence about any method. In two separate runs with different seeds and readout pools, the fixed readout separated a hand-designed history-only control (forced-commit accuracy \DiagForcedInfoMin{} and \StudyForcedInfoMin{}) from the untrained encoder (\DiagForcedRandMax{} and \StudyForcedRandMax{}). One run used a readout pool collected with branching, the other a restore-free pool. The evaluation can therefore detect clue information when it is cleanly present. In the 2$\times$2 study, however, none of the learned encoders reached that point. Their decisions stayed at the untrained encoder's chance level, so the study gives no evidence for either a collection benefit or an action-conditioning benefit. It also gives no evidence against either, because the contrasts sit at a floor. The only quantitative statement the study supports is conditional: with \StudyCfgPretrainBudget{} pretraining transitions, \StudyCfgPretrainEpochs{} epochs at learning rate \StudyCfgPretrainLR{}, and this readout, neither objective nor collection method produced a usable clue representation in three seeds.

\paragraph{Possible explanations (CONJECTURE, not tested).}
The following explanations are consistent with the observations. None has been tested, and we did not change the environment or the settings after seeing the results.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The clue must be carried across the whole corridor, with distractors, while the $k$-step prediction targets depend on it only for windows that start within a few steps of the junction. The training signal for retaining the clue may therefore be weak for both objectives.
  \item The fixed pretraining budget and schedule, taken from an earlier plan without tuning, may be too short for backpropagation through time to establish the long-range dependency.
  \item The reward head may be insensitive to weak clue features. Linear probes reached up to \StudyProbeLearnedMax{} on some learned encoders while forced-commit accuracy stayed at chance.
\end{enumerate}
Distinguishing these explanations would require new runs, which we list as not run in Appendix~\ref{app:planned}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One synthetic task family.} All results come from a small deterministic aliased T-maze. The extreme values of 0.5 and 1.0 reflect this task and should not be expected elsewhere.
  \item \textbf{Reset privilege.} Branching requires a simulator that can save and restore arbitrary states. Any advantage of branching collection would be conditional on that privilege and would not transfer to systems that lack it. Our readout pool is restore-free, but the branching pretraining is not.
  \item \textbf{Three seeds.} Each comparison uses three seeds of one configuration. These are repetitions under a fixed design, not independent replications. A non-significant or null contrast, especially at a floor, is not evidence of equivalence.
  \item \textbf{Forced-commit primary metric.} The primary metric scores one aliased decision after a scripted prefix. It is not a planning success rate, and we make no claim about other policies, horizons, or states outside the data's coverage. Low prediction loss (Table~\ref{tab:study-pretrain}) did not translate into better decisions here, and in general it is not a guarantee of planning performance.
  \item \textbf{Head-dependent readout.} Conclusions are conditional on the frozen-encoder readout: feature standardization, a small reward head, three epoch candidates, and a depth-\DiagCfgPlanHorizon{} planner. A different readout could rank encoders differently. The probe is linear, and its failure is not a proof of absent information.
  \item \textbf{Weak comparison set.} The action-free ablation is not a strong baseline, and our action-conditional variant is not an official reproduction of any published method. We have not compared against an external action-conditioned method such as that of \citet{kwon2024future}, or against recurrent world models \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3,samsami2024r2i}.
  \item \textbf{Untuned pretraining.} The pretraining settings were fixed in advance and not tuned. The negative result may be specific to them.
  \item \textbf{Protocol changes after early looks.} Several protocol choices were revised after smoke runs and control outputs but before any comparison of learned encoders (Appendix~\ref{app:history}).
\end{itemize}
