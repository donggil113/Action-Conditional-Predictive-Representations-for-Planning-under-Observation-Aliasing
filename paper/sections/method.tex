\section{Variants Under Study}
\label{sec:method}

All learned encoders are Elman recurrent networks with $\tanh$ units and hidden size \DiagCfgHidden. The input at time $t$ is the one-hot observation $o_t$, the one-hot previous action, and the previous reward, and the recurrence is $z_t=\tanh(W[x_t;z_{t-1}]+b)$ with $z_{-1}=0$. The predictor is a latent rollout $u_0=z_t$, $u_{j+1}=\tanh(W_d[u_j;\,\alpha_j]+b_d)$, with linear heads for observation logits and reward. Pretraining minimizes the cross-entropy of $o_{t+j+1}$ plus the squared error of $r_{t+j+1}$ for $j<k$. The loss is taken over all $k$-step windows of the main trajectories and over all branch rollouts. After a termination, targets are an absorbing padding symbol with reward zero and remain supervised, because the end of an episode is observable. After a truncation (timeout or budget), targets are masked. The variants differ only in $\alpha_j$ and in the pretraining data.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\acp{} (action-conditional prediction):} $\alpha_j$ is the one-hot action $a_{t+j}$. The objective fits $p(o_{t+j+1}, r_{t+j+1}\mid h_t, a_{t:t+j})$. This conditional target is unconfounded only if the actions in the data do not depend on hidden state. Here the behaviour policy reads only the history, and the continuation policy is uniform.
  \item \textbf{\msp{} (action-free multi-step prediction):} $\alpha_j=0$. The objective fits the action-marginal future under the action distribution of the data, so it depends on the behaviour policy. \msp{} is an \emph{ablation} of \acp{}. It has the same nominal parameter count, but the action columns of $W_d$ receive no gradient, and we do not treat it as a strong or state-of-the-art baseline.
  \item \textbf{Collection:} \emph{branching} data or \emph{no-branching} data under the same transition cap. The difference between them is a difference in collection method, not a controlled comparison on identical data.
  \item \textbf{Reference encoders:} an \emph{untrained} recurrent encoder with the same initialization as the learned encoders; a hand-designed \emph{history-only informative control} whose features are the clue identity read from $o_0$ together with the one-hot current observation; and, in the diagnostic only, a \emph{memoryless control} that sees only the one-hot current observation. The informative control is a reference point for the readout, not an upper bound on what a learned encoder can achieve.
\end{itemize}
Low prediction error is not claimed to guarantee planning performance. The readout and planner above cover one decision, horizon \DiagCfgPlanHorizon{}, and the action support present in the data. Nothing here speaks to other policies, longer horizons, or states outside the data's coverage.
